abstract: |
  Nonrelativistic spin splitting (NRSS) in collinear antiferromagnets offers
  a route to high-frequency spintronics immune to stray fields, but its
  dynamic control has remained elusive. We demonstrate, using density
  functional theory (DFT) and nonlinear phononics, that THz laser pulses can
  achieve ultrafast, reversible control of NRSS on picosecond timescales in
  antiferromagnets. We derive two symmetry criteria, accounting for phonon
  and magnetic wavevector compatibility and order-parameter parity, to
  identify which Raman-active phonon modes can activate or amplify NRSS.
  Applying these rules to NiO and LaFeO₃, we show that resonant driving of
  an infrared-active mode at 11.08 THz transiently converts spin-degenerate
  NiO into an NRSS state via biquadratic anharmonic coupling, generating a
  time-averaged spin splitting of ~40 meV. In LaFeO₃, selective excitation
  amplifies the existing NRSS by about 100%. In both cases, the induced spin
  splitting is accompanied by a transient SOC-induced net moment detectable
  via the magneto-optical Kerr effect. This framework establishes nonlinear
  phononics as a general route for ultrafast manipulation of spin-split
  antiferromagnetic phases well beyond the reach of static strain.
summary: |
